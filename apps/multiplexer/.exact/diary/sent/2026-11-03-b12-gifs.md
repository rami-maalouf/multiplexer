### Rough
- Sending a GIF of 6 MB: the data module fetched it with `arrayBuffer()` and wrote it with `storage.fs.writeFile`. The mutation was refused: "`sendGif` took 344.3 ms of its own (CPU) time ..., over the 100 ms budget for one turn; a long loop can `await scheduler.yield()` every few thousand items". There is no loop to yield in, so I moved the download into the native module (`URLSession`), built it, and it worked. Then, while writing this entry, `contract-for-agents.md` showed `fetch(url, {exactBodyTo: path})`, which writes the body straight to a file and never enters the engine's heap. I removed the native op and used that. About 15 minutes and two Swift builds. What would have helped: the budget refusal naming `exactBodyTo` when the turn's time went into a response body's `arrayBuffer()` (or `writeFile` of one).
- A members file for the GIF sheet could not name the root's `Reply` shape, nor `Composer` and `withComposer`: "naming it here would make a cycle; move it to a file both can use". Workaround, as in an earlier task: a one-field shape of the feature's own (`GifSent`), and a call to the root's `cancelReply()` action in place of the composer update. About 2 minutes.
- A drive holds an animated GIF still: two screenshots 700 ms apart were the same. `--timing platform` with `screenshot <png> window` and `clock +450 real` showed two different frames. About 5 minutes to find; the iOS driving recipe has it.

### Lean in
- `app.json`'s `env` with `import.meta.env.GIF_KEY ?? ''`: a build-time value from a gitignored `.env.local`, and the schema says plainly that it ships in the app.
- A search that waits for a pause is one `task ... key=gifQuery` with `after(400, ...)`; under the agent's clock a drive showed it waiting (300 ms: no search; 800 ms: the search).
- `image` plays an animated GIF from an `app:/cache` path with no extra work.
