### Rough
- The `reply` symbol role is not an arrow: in `kernel/tables/schema.json` it is SF Symbols' `bubble.left`, a speech bubble. An earlier experiment used `image "symbol:reply"` as the swipe's reply arrow and was taken for an arrow, and the open docs change for swipe to reply uses `symbol:reply` for the same arrow. On iOS a reply is the curved arrow (`arrowshape.turn.up.left`); Exact's own `forward` role is already the mirror of it (`arrowshape.turn.up.right`). Cost about 5 minutes of reading the table. I used `symbol:sf/arrowshape.turn.up.left`. `verify ios` then counted it as its one finding (`lower-symbol-platform`: it draws only on Apple, and the app ships web, where it is blank). The warning is right and its fix is documented (an icon token in `app.tamber` with an SVG file), but that is two more files for one arrow that iOS already has, and `scale` takes one number, so the `forward` role cannot be mirrored instead. Left as the warning. What would have helped: a `reply` role that draws the arrow (a `reply-arrow` role, if `reply` must stay a bubble), or a role list that names the symbol each role draws.
- Placing the swipe's indicator: the docs change puts `position="relative"` on the swiped node, but says nothing about why. Without it, an absolute indicator is placed against the nearest positioned ancestor, which in a chat row is the row that also holds the quote above the bubble and the sender's picture, so `top` and `left` measure from the wrong box. Whether a hidden indicator outside the node takes touches was also not written anywhere; I found in `NodeViewIOS.swift` that the host never hit-tests a `swipeIndicator` view. Cost about 5 minutes; no build lost. What would have helped: two lines in the swipe section: the indicator is placed against the swiped node only when that node is positioned, and it never takes a touch.
- The indicator's own `transition` decides how it leaves after the lift. With the swiped node's spring (`-exact-spring(300, 30, 1)`) on both, release frames (`screenshot ... over 400 every 50 window`) showed the arrow's return starting about 50 ms after the bubble's: at 150 ms the bubble was 14 points from rest while the arrow was still at about half opacity, and it passed over a sender's picture beside the bubble. A stiffer spring on the indicator only (`-exact-spring(900, 60, 1)` for opacity and scale) made it small and faint by 100 ms and gone by 200 ms. Cost about 15 minutes and four drives. What would have helped: the indicator's return starting in the same frame as the node's, or the docs saying that it starts later and suggesting a faster spring for it.
- The app is a second git checkout at a different depth from the framework checkout than the first (a tool made the worktree in its own folder). The app names the framework by relative paths in three places (the workspace's `[patch.crates-io]`, and the `apple/` and `web/` crates' dependencies), so from here they point at nothing. `exact.mjs` said "no exact2 at <path>: EXACT2 names the checkout", which was clear, but with `EXACT2` set the build still failed on the workspace's patches, then on `apple/Cargo.toml`. A symlink at the place the relative paths expect did not pass either: the patch check compares the paths without following symlinks (`outsideWorkspaceProblems` uses `resolve`, while `pathFrom` uses `realpathSync`), so it printed the same "must name exact2's vendored ..." refusal. I edited the paths in the three `Cargo.toml` files by hand and kept those edits out of git. Cost about 10 minutes and four builds. What would have helped: `EXACT2` covering the Cargo paths too (for example by writing them into a generated, ignored file), or the patch check resolving symlinks as `pathFrom` does, or the refusal naming the other two files that need the same change.
- At the largest text size (the simulator's accessibility-extra-extra-extra-large), under `--touch platform`, a chat row lay below the screen. `tap avatar-<id> at 22 6` (a point inside a picture in a `link` row, the form that worked at the normal size) refused with "a press at is outside the viewport": the point is missing from the sentence, and a tap at a point does not scroll its target into view first, as a plain tap does. The plain `tap avatar-<id>` then printed "tapped" (it landed on the picture's child), but no chat opened, and the screenshot after it showed the list scrolled to other rows, with the touch's mark on another row's picture. `tap room-<id>` (the `link` itself) opened the chat, but once more it printed "tapped" and opened nothing right after a launch; with `clock +1000 real` before it, it opened the chat every time after that. Cost about 8 minutes and five drives. What would have helped: the refusal naming the point and saying to scroll first (or the tap at a point scrolling too), and a tap that printed "tapped" while the press did nothing saying so (the drive knows the route did not change).

### Lean in
- `swipeIndicator=true` on one child, with that child's own `opacity`, `scale` and `transition`, gave the whole reply arrow: hidden at rest, growing with the drag to 64 points, and leaving on release. No state, no action and no layout change in the app.
- `screenshot <png> over 400 every 50 window` after a real-touch drag made the release measurable frame by frame. That is how the late start of the indicator's return showed.
- `--scale 3` made the arrow's stroke and its gap to the bubble checkable at pixel level.
- A drag aimed at a bubble by its text (`tap "<body>" drag 100 0 from 10 10 ...`) kept working while new messages arrived and changed every bubble's id between launches.
- `check both` after each swipe answered in about a second, in light and dark, and stayed clean at the largest text size.
- `font-size="min(-exact-body, 28px)"` on a symbol let the arrow grow with the reader's text size and stop at a cap, at the first try.
- The CLI's `--json` printed the whole state that a step's `state <name>` cuts at 8 KB, so a `jq` line gave every message's id and body.
